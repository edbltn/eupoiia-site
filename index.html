Eupoiia Index

eu·poi·ï·a /juː ˈpɔɪ i ə/ n.

from Ancient Greek εὐποιΐα (eupoiḯa), from εὖ (eû, “well”) + ποιέω (poiéō, “to do, to make”) + -ία (abstract-noun suffix).

  1. Beneficence; the disposition or practice of doing good to others.
  2. A good deed; a benefaction.

Attested in: Hebrews 13:16 (“τῆς δὲ εὐποιΐας καὶ κοινωνίας μὴ ἐπιλανθάνεσθε”); cf. Liddell–Scott–Jones, s.v. εὐποιΐα.

ancient Israel and Judah

ancient civilization of the Levant

Read moreCollapseWikipedia

Ancient Israel and Judah were two Israelite kingdoms that existed in the Southern Levant during the Iron Age. Their history began with the early appearance of the Israelites in the central highlands of Samaria and Judea. The earliest documented mention of "Israel" as a people appears on the Merneptah Stele, an ancient Egyptian inscription dating back to around 1208 BCE. Archaeological evidence suggests that ancient Israelite culture evolved from the pre-existing Canaanite civilization. During the Iron Age II period, two Israelite kingdoms emerged, covering much of Canaan: the Kingdom of Israel in the north and the Kingdom of Judah in the south.

John Forbes Nash

American mathematician and economist (1928–2015)

Read moreCollapseWikipedia

John Forbes Nash Jr. (June 13, 1928 – May 23, 2015), known and published as John Nash, was an American mathematician who made fundamental contributions to game theory, real algebraic geometry, differential geometry, and partial differential equations. Nash and fellow game theorists John Harsanyi and Reinhard Selten were awarded the 1994 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences. In 2015, he and Louis Nirenberg were awarded the Abel Prize for their contributions to the field of partial differential equations.

Kingdom of Leon

independent medieval kingdom in the northwest of the Iberian Peninsula (910–1230), capital León

Read moreCollapseWikipedia

The Kingdom of León was a medieval state situated in the northwest region of the Iberian Peninsula. It was founded in 910, when the Christian princes of Asturias along the northern coast of the peninsula shifted their capital from Oviedo to the city of León. The kings of León fought civil wars, wars against neighbouring kingdoms, and invasions by the Moors and the Vikings, to sustain their kingdom through a turbulent age.
